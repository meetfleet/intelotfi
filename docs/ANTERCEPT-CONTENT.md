A. PRODUCT-CAPABILITY DEEP-DIVES

CHAPTER 1

Eyebrow: INTELLIGENCE BEFORE DELIVERY
Title: The lure

Antercept builds phishing-awareness exercises around the decisions your people face every day. Its AI turns approved brand context into explainable email and SMS templates, then models likely campaign outcomes before the first message leaves your environment.

Start with the organisation your people recognise

A useful awareness exercise feels relevant to the people receiving it. Antercept studies the brand material you provide: approved messages, vocabulary, formatting conventions and examples of how your organisation communicates. It identifies the details that make a message familiar, from the greeting and sentence length to the way a team signs off.

That context becomes a reusable brand profile. For the fictional Kestrel Works, it might describe concise internal announcements, understated subject lines and a consistent workplace-team signature. Operators can inspect the profile, correct an assumption and decide which characteristics belong in an exercise.

The learning objective sits beside that profile. Verify an unexpected request. Recognise pressure to act quickly. Question a change of channel. Every generated template connects the organisation’s communication style to a specific behaviour the programme intends to practise.

Build one scenario across email and SMS

Antercept composes the scenario for the channel in which it will be encountered. Email gives the exercise room for context, a recognisable sender identity and a structured call to action. SMS compresses the same decision into a shorter exchange, where wording and the apparent reason for contact carry more weight.

The operator chooses the audience, language, scenario and intended difficulty. Antercept produces editable variants with a shared objective, so the team can compare different treatments without losing the purpose of the exercise.

A fictional handbook update might become an email asking employees to review a revised document, alongside an SMS announcing that the review window is closing. Both remain attached to the same campaign brief. Subject lines, message bodies, sender labels and destination previews can be reviewed independently before a version is approved.

Explain the pressure inside the message

Every template includes an explanation of its persuasive elements. Antercept identifies where urgency compresses the recipient’s decision time, where authority gives a request apparent legitimacy, and where familiarity makes the surrounding context feel routine.

Those explanations are attached to specific wording. Select a deadline and see the pressure it introduces. Inspect a sender label and understand the authority it implies. Review a familiar departmental phrase and see why it might reduce scrutiny.

This makes the template useful to the security team before delivery and to the learner afterwards. The same explanation that supports operator review can inform the debrief, connecting the recipient’s experience to a recognisable cue.

Operators can also remove or soften individual elements. The exercise stays aligned with its learning objective while the team controls how much pressure, ambiguity and contextual familiarity it introduces.

Predict the outcome with the assumptions visible

Before launch, Antercept estimates click and report rates using relevant campaign history and the characteristics of the planned exercise. The forecast sits beside the audience and approved template, so operators can examine the expected response in the context of what they are preparing to send.

Each estimate exposes its basis: the comparison campaigns, audience size, observation period and outcome definitions. The interface identifies differences that may weaken the comparison, including a new audience, a different channel or a substantial change in scenario difficulty.

Clicking and reporting remain separate outcomes. A recipient may do both, and the forecast preserves that overlap rather than forcing every response into a single category. Expected counts appear alongside rates, making the operational implications easier to understand.

When comparable evidence is insufficient, Antercept makes that limitation explicit. Operators can explore assumed rates in a separate planning view, with a clear distinction between a model estimate and a manually chosen scenario.

Review a version, then learn from its results

Templates move through a controlled lifecycle. Drafts collect edits and AI suggestions. Review brings the message, audience and destination together. Approval records the exact version prepared for scheduling.

A material edit after approval creates a new review requirement. Active campaigns retain their original message version, preserving the relationship between what recipients encountered and the results the team later examines.

After the exercise, Antercept places observed outcomes beside the pre-launch forecast. Operators can inspect where the response differed and whether the audience, delivery conditions or scenario changed the comparison.

That evidence informs the next campaign. Brand profiles become more useful, template decisions become easier to explain, and planning gains a stronger historical foundation. The programme develops through a repeatable cycle of preparation, observation and revision, with every exercise contributing context to the next.

CHAPTER 2

Eyebrow: FROM INTERACTION TO EVIDENCE
Title: The catch

Antercept organises what a controlled phishing simulation reveals into evidence your security team can investigate. Sessions, cookies, test credentials and recipient actions stay connected to the campaign that produced them, giving every finding a clear operational context.

Keep the interaction attached to its origin

A simulated click is the beginning of an evidence trail. Antercept connects that interaction to the approved message, recipient cohort, destination and campaign version, preserving the context needed to understand what happened.

The evidence view follows the sequence of the exercise. A message is delivered. A recipient opens the training destination. A controlled interaction takes place. A report arrives, or a follow-up lesson begins. Each recorded event has a timestamp and an associated source.

This sequence matters because similar totals can represent different behaviour. Someone who reports before interacting follows a different path from someone who reports afterwards. Antercept keeps those paths visible while aggregating results for the wider campaign.

Security teams can move from the programme overview into a campaign, then into an individual sample record, without losing the filters and context that led them there.

Give sessions a readable structure

Session evidence is organised around the controlled interaction that created it. Operators can inspect when a session began, which simulation destination it belongs to and which events occurred within it.

Where the exercise includes an instrumented training environment, Antercept presents the relevant session artefacts alongside their origin and collection time. The interface distinguishes observed activity from conclusions that require further verification.

A displayed expiry time, for example, describes the recorded lifetime of an artefact. It does not establish that an external service would accept it. That distinction keeps the evidence useful without overstating what the exercise demonstrated.

Related records remain grouped together. The team can inspect a sequence, compare it with the campaign objective and identify the precise point at which a recipient moved from recognising a message to interacting with its destination.

Make cookies and test credentials inspectable

Antercept gives cookie and credential evidence dedicated views within the same investigation. Records retain their association with the simulation environment, the relevant session and the collection event.

Cookie views expose the fields needed to understand the sample artefact, including its name, domain scope and recorded expiry. Test-credential views identify the training account and submission event, with sensitive values masked according to the operator’s access.

Controlled exercises use designated training identities and synthetic secrets. The evidence demonstrates the exposure pathway without requiring employees to submit their everyday passwords. The training experience can record that a submission occurred while keeping the learning objective separate from the collection of unnecessary personal information.

Operators can filter, inspect and annotate these records, then include the relevant evidence in a campaign summary. The result is a structured account of the exercise rather than an unexplained collection of captured fields.

Explain exposure through connected evidence

Antercept’s AI produces an investigation summary from the records available to the security team. It describes the observed sequence, highlights the evidence supporting each statement and identifies gaps that limit the conclusion.

For a fictional campaign at Lumen Grove, the summary might explain that a training destination received a test submission, that the recipient reported the message afterwards and that the assigned lesson remains incomplete. Each statement links back to a corresponding event or metric.

The operator can open those references directly from the summary. If reporting data is unavailable, the explanation identifies the gap instead of treating missing events as a zero.

Recommendations follow the same evidence. A sequence involving an unexpected document request can lead to a lesson on verifying the sender through a known channel. The explanation connects the proposed action to the behaviour observed in the exercise.

Turn the finding into a learning decision

Campaign reporting separates measures that answer different questions. Click rate describes a recorded interaction. Report rate describes use of the reporting path. Lesson completion describes participation in the assigned follow-up.

Antercept shows the denominator behind each measure and accounts for recipients who appear in multiple categories. Repeat clicks do not silently become additional people, and a recipient who clicks and later reports remains visible in both relevant views.

Operators can compare cohorts and successive exercises while retaining the context needed to interpret the comparison. Changes in audience, scenario and delivery conditions stay alongside the results.

The final step is a documented action: assign a lesson, revise a scenario, investigate an unexpected sequence or schedule a follow-up exercise. Evidence remains connected to that decision, giving the security team a clear account of what the programme observed and how it responded.

CHAPTER 3

Eyebrow: CONTROL ACROSS THE PROGRAMME
Title: The map & the machinery

Antercept brings campaign geography, device context and delivery infrastructure into one operational workspace. Your team can prepare, run and review authorised awareness exercises across departments and locations while keeping the supporting systems visible and accountable.

See the operation in its geographical context

Antercept’s map connects campaign activity to the location information available within the authorised exercise. Operators can move between the geographical overview and the underlying records, inspecting the source and timestamp associated with each location.

IP-derived locations are presented as approximate observations. Where an exercise includes an explicitly enrolled test device with a permitted location source, that source remains distinguishable from a network-based estimate.

The map becomes a practical way to explore the programme. Filter by campaign, office, cohort or time window. Select a cluster to inspect its records. Open an event to see how it relates to the wider exercise.

For a fictional organisation such as Northstar Loom, this lets the security team compare activity across its invented offices without treating a map marker as proof of a person’s precise physical position.

Read IP and device context together

Network evidence is easier to interpret when it stays attached to the event that produced it. Antercept brings IP observations, timestamps and available device context into the same investigation path.

An operator can inspect whether a record belongs to the expected training environment, whether several events share a network observation and whether the apparent context changed during the exercise.

Shared infrastructure remains visible in that interpretation. Multiple recipients may appear behind the same network address, while one recipient may generate events from different connections. Antercept preserves those observations without silently equating an address with a unique person.

Filters help teams narrow a large programme to a specific question. Find records from one campaign, inspect a defined time period or compare device categories. Each filtered view retains a route back to the source evidence.

Bring controlled phone data into the case

Phone-data views organise information from enrolled test devices and approved training datasets. The operator can inspect available categories, collection status and the relationship between the dataset and the exercise that authorised it.

A sample backup, device inventory or training-message record appears with its provenance. The interface identifies the source device, collection time and available scope, helping the team understand what the view contains before opening individual records.

Access follows the case. Operators see the datasets relevant to their role, while the activity history records sensitive inspection and export actions.

This gives mobile awareness exercises a consistent place within the programme. An SMS interaction, a test-device event and a follow-up lesson can be reviewed through the same campaign context, with the phone-data view supplying supporting evidence where the exercise includes it.

Manage the infrastructure behind delivery

Antercept connects the programme to the servers, domains and SMS providers used to deliver approved simulations. Each resource has an operational record showing its purpose, ownership and relationship to active campaigns.

Servers expose their configured role and readiness state. Domains remain associated with the training destinations they support. SMS-provider connections show which delivery route a campaign uses and whether that route is available.

Before scheduling, the operator can inspect the dependencies required by the exercise. A campaign with an unavailable destination or an incomplete provider configuration stays visibly unready.

Provider secrets remain masked, with access controlled by role. Operational views focus on connection status, configuration and delivery results, giving campaign owners the information they need without distributing credentials throughout the interface.

The infrastructure becomes part of the campaign record, making later investigation easier when delivery conditions affect the outcome.

Run a programme with clear operational control

Antercept separates the work of preparing an exercise from the authority to release it. Campaign owners assemble the audience and scenario; reviewers inspect the proposed operation; authorised operators schedule and manage delivery.

The programme view brings those states together. Find drafts awaiting review, scheduled exercises, active campaigns and completed work. Search by campaign name, owner, cohort or template, then open the exact version associated with the operation.

Pausing an active campaign stops further dispatches through the controlled workflow. Previously delivered messages remain part of the evidence trail. Resuming preserves that history, so the team can see how the interruption affected the exercise.

As the programme grows, shared templates and infrastructure reduce repeated setup while campaign-specific records preserve accountability. Each exercise retains its own objective, approved audience, delivery configuration and evidence.

The result is a workspace in which strategy and execution stay connected: a training objective becomes an approved operation, infrastructure supports its delivery, and the resulting evidence informs what the organisation practises next.
